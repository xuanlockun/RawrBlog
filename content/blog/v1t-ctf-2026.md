## Setup

### Infrastructure

Unlike 2025, we prepared in advance this year and upgraded from a potato server to a melon server. It still sucked, but at least it did not crash or lag nearly as badly as last year :>

### CTFd

At the time of the event, CTFd gave us two infrastructure problems to think about.

First, although CTFd is designed to be stateless, it can still hold TCP connections. Its live notification feature establishes a connection to the server whenever someone visits the platform. On a weak server, that can become a serious resource problem, so I customized the feature and configured nginx with a very short TTL. This reduced the risk of an attacker abusing long-lived connections to exhaust the server.

Second, unlike last year, I was less worried about CTFd crashing once the infrastructure became more reliable. My bigger concern was running out of connections in the pool. The pool size was based on the number of workers multiplied by 15 maximum connections. With 5 persistent connections, 10 temporary connections, and a 30-second pool timeout, I adjusted the configuration to fit this year’s infrastructure.

## The Prize Incident

After the CTF ended, I went back to my hometown. While I was lying on the train, Frank somehow told me that all of the one-year HTB codes were invalid ;-;

I was terrified. How was I supposed to pay for the prizes? I was broke.

People started coming up with theories and conspiracy plots. Did someone on the team take the codes? What happened?

It turned out that HTB had sent the codes to me quite a while ago, so they had probably used the wrong batch. They sent us valid codes later, and everything was resolved.

Still, not gonna lie, I spent that night on the train feeling like I was lying on a bed of fire.
